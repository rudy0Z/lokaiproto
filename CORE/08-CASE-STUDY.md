## The spine (three acts)

**Act 1 — Ten minutes and no lawyer.** Priya, ₹8,999 mixer, seller refused, no legal vocabulary,
390px phone, maybe Hindi. Most legal tech designs for the lawyer's desk; LokAI designs for the
first ten minutes of a legal problem.

**Act 2 — The assistant that won't guess.** It stops and asks her to confirm the facts before it
will say anything useful. Every claim sits on a visible ledger. At the edge of what it can safely
say it stops, names why, and says what would unblock it. Nothing drafts without her reading it.

**Act 3 — From scripted trust to executable trust.** The first build scripted every trustworthy
moment by hand; an audit caught an out-of-scope question being answered as the consumer case.
The decision layer exists because of that bug. **The drift bug is not hidden — it is the origin
story of the centerpiece**, and the before/after pair is on camera (2026-09-27 walkthrough).

## The thesis, one sentence

> **LokAI builds the seam — the designed boundary where a legal AI stops guessing and starts
> handing off — and it can only do that because it decides before it speaks.**

## The defended positions (argue, never hide)

- **No percentage confidence.** Self-reported percentages are uncalibrated and invite
  over-reliance. `4 of 5 sourced` is checkable; `80%` is theater. Apple's own HIG anchors it.
- **The latency beat.** A CHI '26 paper (n=240) found 2-second answers rated less thoughtful.
  The beat is earned (real stages), narrated, consequence-scaled, predictable (deterministic),
  and skippable — the paper's own ethics prescription. Applied from research, not yet validated
  with users: say that.
- **Determinism.** Not a limitation — the honesty feature: same input, same decision, same beat,
  same receipt. The contract accepts a real model later without changing a user-facing promise.
- **Jev is private scaffolding.** Never in the case study, never in code vocabulary (owner call,
  2026-09-27).
